## Hypothesis

The market is pricing psychedelic therapeutics as a binary binary lottery — high risk of failure, low probability of broad approval — and therefore discounting the entire category at distressed valuations. The mispricing is that the uncertainty is *resolvable* on the 2027 horizon, not structural. COMPASS Phase 3b COMP360 data for treatment-resistant depression arrives 2025-2026; MDMA-assisted therapy is in FDA re-review post-MAPS rejection with a cleaner second application; ketamine/esketamine is already FDA-approved and expanding reimbursement. The market will re-rate the whole category — not just the approved compound — the moment a clean Phase 3 readout lands, because it proves the therapeutic modality, not just the molecule.

## Bull vs. Bear — Steelman Both

**Best bull argument:** Treatment-resistant depression affects ~30% of the 280M depression sufferers globally and has essentially no good second-line pharmacological options. The unmet medical need is so large that even a messy, expensive, therapist-assisted treatment protocol will find coverage — because the alternative (repeated hospitalizations, lost productivity, suicide) costs more. The MAPS rejection was about trial conduct, not safety. COMPASS ran cleaner trials. Once one compound is approved, the DEA is politically pressured to reschedule psilocybin, which unlocks the whole ecosystem.

**Best bear argument:** The reimbursement model is genuinely broken. A psilocybin-assisted therapy session requires 8+ hours of therapist time per patient, multiple sessions, and specialist training. That's $10-20k per treatment course. Even with proof of efficacy, CMS will not cover it at launch — they'll study it for years. The addressable market in the first 3-5 years post-approval is a tiny fraction of the epidemiological TAM, and the companies will need to keep diluting to survive that period.

**Why the long survives the bear:** The bear case is mostly a commercial timing argument, not a thesis invalidator. Stocks will re-rate on clinical proof regardless of the commercial question, because investors will price in eventual access. The optionality is worth owning at current distressed valuations — you're not paying for commercial success, you're paying for clinical optionality. That's cheap.
